# Story devices: what worked, and structures by genre

## Devices from a real production (and why each worked)

**A narrator who is a character.** An unseen old storyteller (a sutradhar, the thread-holder of Indian folk
theatre) who starts dignified, like a retired headmaster, and by the end has lost every argument with a
child. His only line at the very end is the greeting for the occasion. The teller's arc gives adults a second story
and makes the jokes land. Write a casting brief, not a voice name: age, region, register, texture, pace, rules.

**A hidden spine.** Five elements (fire, wind, water, earth, sky), each met by the hero in some chapter. Said
aloud once, near the end, as a verdict ("in four years he has tested all five: five out of five"). Nobody needs
to notice it; it makes the film feel whole. Alternatives: seasons, the senses, firsts, the rooms of a house.

**Three arcs.** The house (empty to full), the
hero (first photo in someone's arms; last shot walking ahead alone; a painted future where he drives and the
father rides behind), the teller (dignified to charmed). Each arc has a first image and a last image; put both
in the treatment.

**A motif that carries time.** A sound: a heartbeat in the dark in the first second that turns out to be the
father's motorbike at the gate. It returns at every chapter break
and at the end as the road. Motifs can also be an object (a helmet that is "winning" one year and "losing" the
next as the child grows), a colour, a gesture.

**Running jokes that escalate.** The child believes the city's festival lights are for him: year 1 "nobody has
had the heart to correct him", year 3 "his belief only got stronger", year 4 "he knows the drums are for him".
Same joke, one step bigger each time. Plant it early; the last one should be the biggest laugh.

**Genre-parody cards.** A streaming-service frame around a home film: a rating card ("advisory: excessive
sweetness, moderate mischief"), "Skip intro: not today", "Are you still watching?", "Episode 5 coming next
year". A rubber-stamped "Laws of the house" card built from the child's real habits, read deadpan. Cheap to
make, loved by adults, and they give the film a shape (episodes, an interval).

**Match cuts across years.** The same yellow shirt and the same flute, one year apart, cut on the same pose: the
child grows up in one beat. Find these in the archive pass (same activity, several years).

**The room as a character.** Questions to the child on screen ("who do you love most?") with 6-8 s of waiting
room; his own recorded answers played back; a sing-along with word highlights on the beat; an interval with a
countdown; the film ending on a live moment (lights up, cake).

**Devices, never material.** Reference masters for their ways of seeing (a realist film's patient wide shots
and natural sound; a fantasy film's rhyming narration; a nonsense poet's ink creatures; a painter's palette)
and never copy their scenes, characters, music, rhymes or lettering. Write per reference: what it brings, where
it leads in the runtime, and what we never copy.

**One borrowed line, sourced.** A short public-domain verse, sourced to a primary text, at the emotional peak.
One is enough; three is a lecture.

## Structures by genre

| Genre | Spine | Opening | Ending | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Birthday / milestone | episodes by year or by element; arcs above | the first image of the story (a heartbeat, a scan, a door) | payoff of the opening + a forward look (a painted future, the next episode card) | cast balance, chronology, kids' attention |
| Memorial / tribute | a life in seasons or in the rooms they lived in | their own voice or their handwriting | the people they leave, their own voice again | never clone the deceased; family decides every sensitive line |
| Wedding / couple | two lines converging (two families, two cities) | a detail from each side | the day itself, then a quiet moment after | former partners, family sensitivities |
| Brand film | founder truth → customer truth → proof → future | the customer's problem in one image | a promise the product can keep (claims ledger) | substantiation for every claim; consent for every face |
| Explainer | status quo → tension → mechanism → proof → action | the misconception or the cost of the problem | one action, said once, shown once | one idea per scene; no jargon without a picture |
| Documentary short | a question → the search → an answer that changes the question | the question as an image | the answer, then a coda | fairness to people on camera; sources for every fact |
| Ad (6-30 s) | hook → proof → brand → CTA | the hook in the first 2-3 s, sound off readable | brand + CTA held long enough to read | mandatory supers and durations (see `marketing-video`) |

## The emotional rhythm (minute by minute)

Plot the film as a line: tension and release alternate every 40-90 s. A long emotional stretch needs a laugh to
let the room breathe; a long comic stretch needs one quiet image. Put the biggest emotional beat at ~80-85 % of
the runtime and the biggest laugh after it. End within 60-90 s of the last emotional peak.

## Narration voice for different products

- Personal film, narrator character: third person, specific, dry, affectionate, no adjectives.
- Personal film, parent's voice: first person to the child ("you"), short, no performance.
- Brand film: the customer's language, not the brand's; one claim per line, each in the claims ledger.
- Explainer: active verbs, concrete nouns, numbers said as people say them, one idea per sentence.
