# Client inbox

Create `inbox/` (or a folder on the client's own drive) with numbered subfolders. Each gets a README.txt with
the same four lines, so the client never has to ask what goes where:

```
WHAT GOES HERE: <e.g. village evening shots: the pond, the house, flowers in the lane>
USED IN:        <scene / chapter>
BY WHEN:        <date>  (after this date we build with what we have)
HOW:            <phone sideways, held still 10-15 s, no zoom, no talking; best light 10 minutes before dark>
```

Typical folders: 01_people-photos-missing, 02_sounds (hums, songs, ulu, prayers), 03_letters-or-wishes,
04_location-shots, 05_voice-notes (the client telling the story, any language), 06_documents, 07_blessings
(one line each to camera), 08_anything-else.

## Phone recording guides (paste into the README)

**A hum, a lullaby, a song**: quiet room, fan and TV off, phone about 30 cm away, two takes, slowly. A family
voice beats any professional recording for these moments.

**Ritual sounds (conch, bell, ululation)**: a quiet room, phone about 1 m away, three takes (one person alone,
then two or three together the way it sounds at a ceremony). Library recordings of the "same" sound are often
from another culture and sound wrong.

**Voice notes telling the story**: talk to the phone like telling a friend, any language, one note per year or
event. These are transcribed and the narration is written from the client's own words.

**Location shots**: phone sideways, held still or leaning on a wall, 10-15 s each, no zoom, no talking; the
ten minutes before dark are the best light.

**Handwritten letters**: black pen on plain white paper, write big; photograph from straight above in daylight,
no phone shadow. (Typed letters can be set in hand lettering instead; never edit the words.)

**Someone speaking to camera**: phone sideways at eye level, window light in front of them, never behind;
ask, then stay silent until they finish; laugh after, not over them; five minutes at a time at most.
